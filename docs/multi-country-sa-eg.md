## Proposed normalized model

`organizations → markets → branches/resources/configuration`. A market has a stable UUID, unique organization/country code, AR/EN names, currency, IANA timezone, calling code, default locale and activation state. SA/SAR/Asia-Riyadh and EG/EGP/Africa-Cairo are catalogue configuration, not scattered business switches. Configuration is privileged; customers cannot write or activate it. Locale continues to determine language/direction only.

Country-scoped regions and cities use stable IDs and localized names. Market service coverage references cities separately from the catalogue. An inactive/unconfigured location cannot be submitted merely because its name is known. Initial catalogue coverage is deliberately limited and extensible; it is not a national service declaration. Existing service types can be retained with market availability relations rather than duplicating their identity unnecessarily.

Requests have a non-null market. At creation the customer explicitly selects an active market; the selection is immutable for that draft. Changing country means explicitly cancelling/restarting a draft, with no carried prices or attachments. This is stricter than the safe editable boundary and avoids accidentally carrying addresses, tax or service selection. Existing legacy drafts receive SA context; they must choose valid structured locations before new submission. Previously submitted addresses remain historical text with nullable structured IDs, never fabricated mappings.

Commercial and operational records inherit market with composite tenant/market/parent FKs. Currency and applied tax facts are immutable snapshots. Market currency cannot be switched under existing transactions. Pricing/tax selectors require market equality and effective validity; new tax versions never reinterpret sent/accepted records. A missing applicable tax/pricing configuration fails closed. Synthetic Egyptian tariffs/tax are allowed only in guarded Staging fixtures and clearly labelled as such.

## Migration/backfill and compatibility

Add new catalogue/configuration relations and nullable context columns first. Deterministically create SA context for each existing organization with Saudi-origin records/configuration, then derive child context from its parent. EG configuration belongs to the same Naql365 organization; no Egyptian historical data is created. Do not create markets for every future tenant automatically merely because an organization is inserted.

Backfill original amounts and tax values unchanged. Old immutable snapshots receive only new context; any migration-specific handling of existing immutability triggers must be confined to the migration transaction, restored before commit, and tested against old/new commercial facts. Do not use an application-visible maintenance bypass. Make new relationships non-null only after deterministic backfill. Unknown legacy city strings remain unchanged; structured IDs are required for new domestic commands, not invented for historical rows.

Keep the thirteen accepted migrations unchanged. Add explicit replacement command definitions in new migrations rather than editing historical files. Preserve RPC signatures where practical but reject missing country on new public creation. Adapt tests to supply explicit markets instead of retaining an unsafe implicit-SA creation fallback. Preserve all previous customer, idempotency, expiry, resource conflict, stop dependency, signature and completion invariants.

## Time and references

Use `Africa/Cairo`, verified against [IANA timezone documentation](https://data.iana.org/time-zones/tz-link.html). Runtime/database tzdata must handle DST; no homemade seasonal offsets. Timestamps remain timestamptz. Local dates/time windows remain preferences in the request market. Trip local input must be resolved in the market zone; reject nonexistent wall times and require explicit disambiguation for repeated wall times rather than silently choosing an offset. Reference month uses market-local current time while global counters preserve uniqueness across markets. Old references never change. Test winter/summer, DST transitions and distinct SA/EG month boundaries using real zone rules.
