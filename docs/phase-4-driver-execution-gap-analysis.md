## Execution design

Driver Start Trip maps to existing `dispatch` (READY required). Subsequent commands are `arrive`, `start_service`, `complete_stop`, `depart`, and `complete_trip`. Planning, readiness review, assignment/reassignment, vehicle changes, terminal fail/cancel and fleet management remain staff authority. No generic status setter or additional scheduled-time restriction is introduced: READY is the existing dispatch boundary, including an upcoming Trip explicitly made ready by Operations.

Authentication, role, membership, internal resource, tenant/market and current assignment are verified for every command. Authorization runs under the existing organization advisory lock before reading mutation replay, so old-driver retries after reassignment cannot regain access. Identical successful final-completion retries may return their stored result for the final driver while their current identity authorization remains valid; they cannot mutate completed history. Revision conflicts and different-intent mutation reuse remain errors.

Today includes assigned unfinished Trips scheduled on or before today's market-local date (overdue work remains visible); Upcoming includes future market-local dates. Completed is bounded/paginated and read-only, attributed to the final assignment, not any historical driver who once saw the Trip. Unscheduled assigned work is explicitly identified in Today. Cancelled/failed Trips are not presented as completed deliveries. Active-assignment detail alone may expose the Request's operational contact name/phone and planned Stop address; it never joins the full customer profile/history for display. Changing membership/resource/assignment removes those details on the next server read.

Issue reporting records a private operational concern with a mandatory bounded reason and one optional normalized private image. It does not silently finish a Stop, fail/cancel a Trip or change commercial state. Operations can resolve the attention flag with an audited reason and use existing state commands; financial/legal resolution and rescheduling remain deferred. Reassignment preserves issue history and gives the new currently authorized driver only the operational issue context needed to continue. Existing dispatcher-private notes remain hidden.

Location is optional evidence, not proof of physical presence. Only user-triggered arrival/final-POD capture uses one `getCurrentPosition` call. No background timers, live route history, analytics coordinates or routing credentials. Failed location capture shows a localized unavailable/denied message and the action remains usable. Browser-supplied location never chooses the actor, Trip, Stop or market association.

## Provisioning, files and audit

Trusted operators create/invite a controlled Auth identity, validate the intended organization/market/resource, insert driver-category membership plus DRIVER role, and link the existing INTERNAL resource by UUID in an administrative transaction. No email text/metadata matching, no client role promotion and no service-role key in the application. A resource without a profile remains staff-operated; EXTERNAL remains unauthenticated and excluded even if legacy/corrupt mapping is attempted.

POD signatures and issue photos remain private. Existing generic `file_objects` ownership grants are unsuitable for issue evidence after reassignment; use an issue-linked private reservation with no generic owner bypass, current authorization checks, bounded normalized PNG/JPEG bytes, no overwrite and short signed URLs. Required metadata and immutable records stay database guarded. No antivirus or certified digital-signature claim. Raw issue/geolocation/audit access is not granted to customers or general driver accounts; safe database projections and narrowly authorized file helpers enforce access.

Events retain authenticated actor and an explicit DRIVER/STAFF source without copying email, phone, addresses or exact coordinates into generic audit metadata. Domain location fields associate to the event. No existing analytics SDK is present; authoritative operational events are sufficient for Phase 4, without adding a tracking provider.
